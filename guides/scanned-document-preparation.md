# Scanned PDF to Excel: prepare and review your document

Readable source pages make document data easier to capture and review. When converting a scanned PDF to Excel, or working with a photo of a table, check the complete page and its smallest text.

## Keep the full document visible

- Include all page edges, column headings, and totals.
- Place the page flat and keep the camera facing it directly.
- Use even lighting and avoid shadows, reflections, and fingers covering text.
- Rotate sideways pages so the text reads normally.
- Keep pages in their original order, including continuation pages.

Save the original image before making adjustments. If a crop has already removed a column or a total, take a new photo or scan of the complete page.

## Check detail before uploading

Zoom in on a small amount, a date, and a long reference number. If those characters are difficult to distinguish, improve the source rather than relying on a later correction.

Use a direct PDF or image export when available. Repeated screenshots and compressed copies can make decimal points, minus signs, and small characters harder to read.

## Review common trouble spots

| Source issue | What to inspect in the spreadsheet |
| --- | --- |
| Skewed or curved lines | Values may move into a neighboring column. |
| Faint text or stamps | Digits and parts of descriptions may be missing. |
| Cropped page edges | The first or last column may be incomplete. |
| Repeated headings | A heading may appear among the data rows. |
| Wrapped descriptions | One item may split into multiple rows. |
| Similar-looking characters | Check zeros, the letter O, ones, and the letter I in identifiers. |

Compare the row count and important values with every source page. Treat a blank cell as something to investigate, rather than proof that the original field was empty.
